Plans that changed

  • Flight delayed Weather held up our Hong Kong to Haneda flight, so we landed around 3 PM.
  • Morning lost A “quick” shopping stop swallowed the morning, so Kamakura came late.
  • Rain, plan B Rain in Kyoto: Kiyomizu-dera and Nanzen-ji skipped, teamLab instead.
  • A detour We visited the Harry Potter studio before leaving Tokyo for Kyoto.

Tokyo

We landed at Haneda around 3 PM after the weather delay. I had filled in Visit Japan Web beforehand, so immigration was quick. First night: ramen and a drink. Next day: Senso-ji and the streets around it, Don Quijote for tax-free shopping, Ueno Park, a six-storey Akihabara building full of anime figures and gadgets, Uniqlo in Ginza, then Shibuya for Hachiko, the famous crossing and dinner.

Before leaving for Kyoto we squeezed in the Harry Potter studio tour (official tickets (opens in new tab)). A wonderful time, but beware of impulsive shopping.

    Hakone and Mt Fuji

    A full day on a guided Hakone and Mt Fuji tour. How I booked it and what it cost is on the Stay & Travel page.

      Kamakura

      I planned to leave at 10 AM, but a quick shopping stop ate the morning. We reached Kamakura late and saw Kotoku-in and Hase-dera. Temples close at 5 PM and the sun sets around 6, so there is little to do after that. We still enjoyed what we saw.

        Kyoto

        After the studio we took the train to Kyoto. At Nishiki Market the shops were closing around 10 PM, so we had ramen and walked Gion's Hanamikoji Street instead.

        The next day rained from morning, but we did not come all this way to sit in a hotel. Tofuku-ji and the Tsuten-kyo bridge, then Fushimi Inari, which was lovely in the drizzle. We also had our first Indian lunch of the trip: not great, and the most expensive meal so far. Kiyomizu-dera and Nanzen-ji had to go, because we were getting wet. We went back, changed plans, and bought teamLab tickets on the spot: an amazing, immersive experience.

          Nara

          Another rainy day. It was fascinating to see so many well-mannered deer bowing for a treat. Todai-ji is a massive wooden structure, built from huge tree trunks, with a giant Buddha inside.

            Osaka

            We checked in, then walked to Osaka Castle and its park, all within walking distance of the hotel. That night we explored Don Quijote and the streets of Namba. The next day was a relaxed one at the Kaiyukan Aquarium.

              What I learned

              1. Start early. Temples close around 5 PM and the sun sets around 6.
              2. Rain is not a reason to stay in. Carry an umbrella and go anyway.
              3. Book the Harry Potter studio ahead, on the official site (opens in new tab).
              4. Nishiki Market shops close early, so go in the afternoon.
              5. teamLab tickets bought on the spot worked for us, but check availability first.
